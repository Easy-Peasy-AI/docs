A routine is a standing order: work an employee does on its own while you're away. It runs on a schedule, such as a morning brief every weekday at 8, or when something happens: another service calls its webhook, or an email arrives in the employee's Gmail or Outlook inbox. Each run follows the routine's instructions with the employee's usual tools, apps, memory, and knowledge.

Routines run on our servers, so they work with your laptop closed. Every run is in the routine's history, and its result lands in a conversation.

## Create a routine by asking

Tell the employee what you want and when, in its direct message:

> Every weekday at 8am, check yesterday's orders in Stripe and send me a three-line summary.

The employee sets up the routine with clear instructions and a name, and tells you when it runs next. It runs in your time zone. Ask for a one-time job the same way: "On Friday at 4pm, remind me to send the boutique price list."

Employees can create routines when you ask, but only you can change, pause, or delete them, on the **Routines** page. For a routine that starts from a webhook or an email, use the page too.

  <Step title="Choose when it runs">

## What can start a routine

### On a schedule

Pick **Every weekday at 8:00**, **Every day at 8:00**, **Every Monday at 9:00**, **Every hour**, or **Custom cron…**. A custom schedule is a cron expression with six fields: second, minute, hour, day of the month, month, and day of the week. `0 30 8 * * 1-5` means 8:30 on weekdays.

Times are in your browser's time zone when you create the routine. A routine runs at most every 5 minutes.

### When a webhook is called

A webhook routine runs each time another service calls its private URL: Stripe when an order is paid, Shopify when an order comes in, a form on your website, Zapier or Make, or your own code. The employee gets the data that was sent, as JSON, form fields, or text, along with its instructions.

After you create the routine, its row shows the webhook URL. Copy it with the button next to it and paste it into the other service's webhook settings. **Example request** shows a test call you can send from a terminal.

Anyone with the URL can start the routine, so keep it private. If it leaks, click **Reset URL** and confirm. The old URL stops working right away.

- A call can carry up to 256 KB.
- A routine takes up to 60 calls a minute, and up to 100 calls can wait their turn. Calls beyond that are refused with a "too many requests" error.
- If the other service sends the same event twice, with the same delivery ID (such as Stripe's event ID or an `Idempotency-Key` header), it runs once.
- While the routine is paused, calls are refused.

### When an email arrives

An email routine runs when a new email arrives in the Gmail or Outlook inbox [connected to this employee](/ai-employees/apps). Connect the inbox in **Apps** first, then pick it under **Inbox**.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Only emails from (optional)** | Addresses or domains, separated by commas, such as `stripe.com, anna@client.com`. |
| **Subject contains (optional)** | Words, separated by commas, such as `refund, invoice`. |
| **Also run on newsletters, receipts and other automated emails** | Off by default, so those emails are skipped. Auto-replies and delivery reports never start a run. |

The inbox is checked every 2 minutes, and only emails that arrive after you create the routine start a run. Emails that arrive while the routine is paused are skipped. The routine's row shows when the inbox was last checked.

Grok and DeepSeek models can't read Gmail, so an employee on one of those models can't have a Gmail routine.

## Write good instructions

Nobody is there to answer questions during a run, so the instructions have to stand on their own. When the employee creates a routine for you, it writes them this way. When you write them yourself, include:

- **The goal and who it's for**: "Write three Instagram post ideas for Aurela's Bloom launch."
- **Where to look**: the websites, files, or apps to use.
- **What to do with what arrives**: for a webhook or email routine, say how to use the data. "Read the email. If it's a question about an order, look it up in Shopify and draft a reply in our tone. If it's a refund request, tell me who asked and why."
- **How to avoid repeats**: "Check the earlier runs and don't repeat an idea."
- **The format of the result**: a table, three bullets, a one-page report.

## Test a routine

Each routine's row has a test button:

| Routine | Button | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On a schedule | **Run now** | Runs it now, without moving its schedule. Works on a paused routine too. |
| Webhook | **Send a test event** | Sends sample data, marked as made up, and runs the routine on it. |
| Email | **Test with the latest email** | Runs the routine on the latest email that matches its settings. |

The result shows up in a minute or two.

## Where results go

- **Scheduled routines** post their results in the employee's [direct message](/ai-employees/direct-messages).
- **Webhook and email routines** post in a conversation of their own, named "Routine:" followed by the routine's name, so a burst of events never gets in the way of your chat with the employee. Open it from **Conversations** in the direct message.

Click **Show run history** on a routine's row to see its last runs, and for a webhook or email routine, its recent events and whether each one was handled.

### Emails about routines

- After each run of a **scheduled** routine, you get an email with a link to the result.
- A **webhook or email** routine emails you only when it starts failing, once, not after every failed run.

### Approvals during a run

Nobody watches a run, so when a routine wants to send an email, change your calendar, or change something in an app, the action waits in your **Approvals** and you get an email. The run finishes without it. See [Approvals](/ai-employees/approvals#when-nobody-is-watching).

### Memory during a run

A webhook or email routine reads the employee's memory but can't change it, because the data that starts it comes from outside and could be written by anyone. Scheduled routines can update memory like a chat.

## Manage a routine

The buttons on a routine's row:

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Pause**, **Resume** | Stops it without deleting it, and starts it again. |
| **Edit** | Changes the name, the instructions, the schedule, or an email routine's filters. To change what starts it, create a new routine. |
| **Delete** | Click it, then **Delete?** to confirm. |

A routine runs on the employee's current model, so changing the employee's [quality](/ai-employees/profile#quality) changes its routines too. While the employee is paused, its routines don't run.

Your employees' routines also show on Marky's [Automations](/marky/automations) page, with the employee as **Run by**.

## Limits

- You can have up to 10 routines and automations in all, counting Marky's automations and paused ones. Delete one to add another.
- A schedule runs at most every 5 minutes.
- A webhook or email routine runs at most 20 times an hour. More events wait their turn, and one run can handle up to 5 that arrived together. An event that waits more than a day is skipped.
- An inbox check takes up to 20 new emails. If more arrive between two checks, the rest are skipped.
- Up to 3 webhook and email runs work at the same time across your account.

## Credits

Each run uses words like any other message to the employee, at the rate of its model, plus image & video credits for anything it generates. Test runs count too. Frequent schedules and busy webhooks add up, so check [Usage](/account/usage) after the first few runs.
